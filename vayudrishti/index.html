The idea

Different weather needs
different corrections.

The proposed system identifies the weather conditions affecting each small area, selects suitable correction models, and recalibrates forecast probabilities.

01 / WEATHER-AWARE

Recognize local regimes

Distinguish dry conditions, depressions, orographic rainfall and ordinary monsoon weather, including mixed conditions.

02 / ADAPTIVE

Blend the right correction

Use regime-specific experts and learn from local forecast errors to produce a corrected rainfall forecast.

03 / CONFIDENCE-AWARE

Know when to abstain

Generate local warning maps and high-confidence alerts. Withhold an automated warning when confidence is low.

Technical approach

A four-stage pipeline.

Explore the planned path from weather inputs to district warnings.

Combine forecast and context.

The deck proposes NCUM numerical rainfall forecasts, IMD gridded rainfall observations, ERA5 wind, humidity and sea-level pressure, SRTM terrain elevation, and the CPC Niño-3.4 index as inputs.

NWP / NCUMIMD rainfallERA5 atmosphereSRTM terrainCPC Niño-3.4

Identify the weather situation.

AI classifies local weather into dry, depression, orographic and ordinary monsoon regimes. Mixed conditions are possible, so the workflow allows more than one correction expert to contribute.

DryDepressionOrographicOrdinary monsoonMixed regimes

Learn from local forecast errors.

A regime-based post-processing engine blends depression, monsoon and orographic experts. The deck identifies EMOS (Ensemble Model Output Statistics) as part of this correction approach.

Regime-specific expertsBlended forecastEMOSLocal error correction

Turn probabilities into cautious warnings.

Planned outputs include heavy-rain and very-heavy-rain probabilities, confidence-aware warning maps, and high-confidence SMS alerts. Low-confidence cases abstain from automated warning.

Rainfall probabilityLocal warning mapsHigh-confidence alertsLow-confidence abstention
A proposal, not an operational warning service.

This project website presents the team's concept. It does not issue real-time weather alerts, show measured accuracy, or replace official forecasts. Withholding a warning does not mean an area is safe.

Impact & benefits

Designed for local preparedness.

The intended outcome is earlier identification of rainfall-risk areas to support disaster preparedness and planning.

  • Timely community alerts and awareness of local rainfall risk.
  • Better crop and infrastructure planning to help reduce avoidable losses.
  • Localized risk maps to support flood and water-management planning.

Intended benefits from the proposal; not measured project results.

Feasibility

Modular by design.

  • Public datasets: weather, rainfall and terrain sources identified in the deck.
  • Lightweight processing: designed for normal computing hardware.
  • Separate modules: improve classification, correction and warnings independently.
  • Grid-based workflow: planned extension to larger regions.
  • Practical integration: a dashboard and district warning system are proposed destinations.

Project notes

What this page represents.

Is this a working forecast dashboard?

No. This is a project showcase of the proposed system. No real-time feeds, generated forecasts or active alert subscriptions are connected.

What does regime-aware mean here?

Correction depends on the local weather situation rather than applying the same correction everywhere. The deck describes separate depression, monsoon and orographic experts, with blending for mixed conditions.
